# ADR-005: Roll low stock up the category tree with no fixed depth

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-27
- **Decision owner:** Yaa Antwi

## Context

Categories nest three or four levels deep and marketing keeps adding more. The brief says not to hard-code the depth. The data also has two categories that point at each other, and one that names a parent that does not exist. The brief does not say what "low" means.

## Decision

Use a query that walks down the tree one level at a time until it runs out of levels (a recursive query). Its safety limit is the number of categories in the table, so it never cuts off a real level. Each category shows its own products and everything beneath it. Products in the loop, under a missing parent, or with no category show on their own lines so none are lost.

Out of stock means 0 or fewer units. Low means above 0 and below 5. The 5 sits in a small settings table, and one command changes it.

## Alternatives considered

### Alternative A: Join the table to itself a fixed number of times

- **How it would work:** One join per level.
- **Why it is attractive:** Simple to read.
- **Why rejected:** It breaks the day marketing adds a fifth level, which the brief warns about.

### Alternative B: A limit based on sales speed ("under 7 days of stock left")

- **How it would work:** Flag a product if it would sell out within a week at its recent pace.
- **Why it is attractive:** It is smarter in principle.
- **Why rejected:** I measured it. On your data it flagged 0 products beyond what a small-stock rule already catches.

### Alternative C: Fewer than 10

- **How it would work:** A wider net.
- **Why it is attractive:** Fewer surprises.
- **Why rejected:** It flags 48% of products. People stop reading a list that long.

## Consequences

- **What this buys:** The real tree has 4 levels and works. On a practice copy, a chain of 120 levels was read to the end. On your machine, the top-level lines plus the two extra lines add up to all 4,023 products, 591 out of stock and 833 low (checks passed). 91 of 94 categories are reached from the top.
- **What this costs:** 1,424 flagged products is 35% of the catalogue, probably too long to act on in one go. The out-of-stock products sit in their own column, so Nana can read those first. The 3 categories that cannot be reached, and the products under them, appear only on the "Unreachable" line.
- **What it costs the client if I am wrong:** A long list may get ignored (one command lowers it to 2, which flags 548 low). A product in the wrong category is counted in the wrong branch; the correct category for the 43 affected products needs confirming.
- **What would make me revisit this:** Nana saying the list is too long, or a supplier delivery time longer than the stock lasts. **Who decides:** Nana for the cut-off, and marketing for the correct categories.

## What this survives

The tree growing, and a flash sale: a sale can use 3 to 5 units in seconds, so a threshold of 5 gives some warning where 2 would give almost none. The view read in a few thousandths of a second on a practice copy.
